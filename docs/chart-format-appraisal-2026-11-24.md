# Chart sources and formats for SDL2 Plotter: appraisal

Date: 2026-11-24. Research against primary sources (hydrographic office pages, format specifications, crates.io, GitHub repositories, OpenCPN, Signal K and Freeboard-SK documentation and source). Every factual claim carries a link. Anything not stated directly by a source is marked **(estimate)** or **(unverified)**; claims taken from a search-engine summary of a page rather than from the fetched page itself are marked **(secondary)**. S-57, S-52 and S-63 internals, NOAA ENC licensing, German IENC and o-charts encryption are covered in the sibling [S-57 ENC feasibility study](s57-enc-feasibility-2026-11-24.md) and are referenced, not repeated. Crate versions and dates come from the crates.io API on 2026-11-24.

## Summary

**(a) What is genuinely free and legal worldwide.** Free official *charts* exist in only a handful of places: the United States (NOAA ENC, plus NOAA's own ENC-derived raster MBTiles), New Zealand and its Pacific realm (LINZ), Brazil (BSB raster, non-commercial use), Argentina (BSB rasters, now only as 2022 archive copies), Peru (13 BSB rasters) and the European inland waterways (IENC, S-57, free from more than a dozen states). Canada, Australia, the UK, France, Croatia and Portugal sell their sea charts and give away at most a sample cell or historical scans. Everywhere else the free and legal layers are *not* charts: OpenSeaMap seamarks (ODbL data), OpenStreetMap land polygons (ODbL), Natural Earth (public domain), GEBCO 2026 (15 arc-second global grid) and EMODnet bathymetry (about 115 m grid, European seas). Harvested Google, Bing or Esri imagery is not legal to cache offline under those providers' terms, and circulating CM93 v2 files are unlicensed copies of copyrighted C-Map data. The cheap legal route outside the free countries is o-charts (Portugal with Madeira and the Azores: 16 EUR excl. VAT), which is encrypted and OpenCPN-only.

**(b) Their formats.** Raster sources arrive as BSB/KAP (Brazil, Argentina, Peru, the NZ archive, US pilot charts, OpenSeaMap's offline set) or GeoTIFF (SHOM, GEBCO, EMODnet), and NOAA also publishes ready-made raster MBTiles. Vector sources arrive as S-57 (NOAA, LINZ, IENC), OSM PBF (OpenSeaMap), shapefile (OSM land polygons, Natural Earth, GSHHG) or netCDF/GeoTIFF grids (bathymetry). The community already repackages much of this: the OpenCPN chart-downloader catalogue lists 27 free sources and its bot updated it on the day of this study.

**(c) What pure Rust reads today.** Mature and pure Rust: PMTiles v3 read *and* write (`pmtiles` 0.28.1), MVT decode (`mvt-reader` 2.5.0, `geozero` 0.15.1) and encode (`mvt` 0.15.0, `geozero`), TIFF pixels (`tiff` 0.11.3; `geotiff` 0.1.0 for georeferencing is immature), OSM PBF (`osmpbf` 0.3.8), shapefile (`shapefile` 0.9.0), FlatGeobuf (`flatgeobuf` 6.0.1), GeoJSON (`geojson` 1.0.0), JPEG (`zune-jpeg`), PNG (`png`) and lossy and lossless WebP decode (`image-webp`), the last three as defaults of `image` 0.25.10. MBTiles and GeoPackage need SQLite, which is C, but the app already compiles it in through bundled `rusqlite`, so neither adds a runtime dependency. There is no pure-Rust BSB/KAP reader (the `bsb` crate on crates.io is an unrelated protocol codec); the format is small and documented by GDAL's MIT-licensed reader, so an in-house reader is a few hundred lines **(estimate)**. There is no pure-Rust equivalent of tippecanoe, but every part an importer needs to tile vectors itself (clipping, simplification, MVT encoding, PMTiles or MBTiles writing) exists as a crate.

### 1.2 Free open data that is not a chart

- **OpenSeaMap.** Licences "CC BY-SA (map), ODbL (data)" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenSeaMap)) **(secondary)**. Seamarks are ordinary OSM objects tagged `seamark:type=*`, so a Geofabrik-style `.osm.pbf` extract is the legal bulk source. OpenSeaMap also offers "more than 300 sea and river charts" as KAP and other formats ([OpenSeaMap offline charts](https://www.openseamap.org/index.php?id=offlinekarten)) **(secondary)**. The seamark tile layer has no published bulk-download policy ([OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Raster_tile_providers)); the absence of a policy is not permission. Open Waters publishes dated whole-planet seamark PMTiles at `tiles.openwaters.io/seamap/<YYYY-MM-DD>.pmtiles`, tiles CC BY 4.0, code GPL-3.0 ([openwatersio/seamap](https://github.com/openwatersio/seamap)) **(secondary)**.
- **OSM land and coastline polygons.** Shapefiles in WGS84 or Web Mercator, "complete" or "split" ("use for larger scales") ([osmdata land polygons](https://osmdata.openstreetmap.de/data/land-polygons.html)). Derived from OSM, hence ODbL ([osmdata licence](https://osmdata.openstreetmap.de/info/license.html)) **(licence page not fetched)**.
- **Natural Earth.** "All versions of Natural Earth raster + vector map data ... are in the public domain", at 1:10m, 1:50m, 1:110m ([terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)).
- **GEBCO.** The current release is **GEBCO_2026** (the brief named 2025): "a global terrain model for ocean and land ... on a 15 arc-second interval grid", as global netCDF (4 GB zipped, 7.0 GB uncompressed) or eight 90x90 degree tiles in GeoTIFF or Esri ASCII, plus a custom-area download app ([GEBCO](https://www.gebco.net/data-products/gridded-bathymetry-data)). GEBCO's terms of use put the grid in the public domain **(unverified this session)**. A GEBCO 2024 shaded-relief MBTiles built for Signal K is catalogued ([GEBCO_MBTiles_Catalog.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chartcatalogs/catalogs/master/GEBCO_MBTiles_Catalog.xml)). 15 arc-seconds is about 460 m at the equator **(estimate)**: a background for offshore passages, not a pilotage depth source.
- **EMODnet Bathymetry DTM 2024.** 1/16 arc-minute grid (about 115 m), depths to LAT, 59 tiles in GeoTIFF, NetCDF, ESRI ASCII, XYZ and more, published 2024-12-31 ([EMODnet](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/bathymetry), [tile H8](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/geonetwork/srv/api/records/914b2858-7277-4efd-a4d1-337210629089)) **(secondary)**. Licence CC BY 4.0 **(unverified)**.
- **NOAA NCEI** coastal DEMs and multibeam data: not researched in this session **(unverified)**.
- **World basemaps for OpenCPN**: GSHHG and OSM-derived shapefiles ([GSHHG_Catalog.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chartcatalogs/catalogs/master/GSHHG_Catalog.xml), [OSMSHP_Catalog.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chartcatalogs/catalogs/master/OSMSHP_Catalog.xml)); US pilot charts as KAP ([PILOT_Catalog.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chartcatalogs/catalogs/master/PILOT_Catalog.xml)); a Signal K world coastline MBTiles ([netAction/signalk-world-coastline-map](https://github.com/netAction/signalk-world-coastline-map)).

### 1.3 Satellite imagery and harvested tiles

- **Google** forbids customers to "pre-fetch, index, store, reshare, or rehost Google Maps Content outside the services" or "bulk download Google Maps tiles" ([Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)) **(secondary)**, and its FAQ names "the use of a bulk tile download script" ([FAQ](https://developers.google.com/maps/faq)) **(secondary)**.
- **Bing**: "You agree not to ... copy, cache, or retain any maps, routes, images, or other data provided to you by Microsoft" ([Bing Maps end-user terms](https://www.microsoft.com/maps/assets/docs/terms.aspx)) **(secondary)**. Free Bing Maps for Enterprise accounts ended on 2025-06-30, enterprise accounts end on 2028-06-30 ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4371917/requirement-for-offline-map-functionality-using-bi)) **(secondary)**.
- **Esri**: World Imagery "is not intended to be used to export tiles for offline"; the export layer needs an ArcGIS subscription and is for ArcGIS applications ([World Imagery](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=10df2279f9684e4a9f6a7f08febac2a9), [for Export](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=226d23f076da478bba4589e7eae95952)); Wayback exports "are intended for use only within ArcGIS" ([Wayback Export](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/arcgis-living-atlas/imagery/wayback-export)) **(secondary)**.
- **SAS.Planet-style harvests**, which is what the owner's current charts are, copy those services outside their terms. SAS.Planet exports "MBTiles 1.2 (SQLite)", RMaps, OruxMaps, OsmAnd, KMZ and JNX ([SASPlanet help, French](https://www.hisse-et-oh.com/store/medias/sailing/5dd/21e/b63/original/5dd21eb639819f3668e0e0e6.pdf)) **(secondary)**. The plotter can read what a user already holds, but should not ship, document or automate harvesting.

**BSB/KAP in detail**, from GDAL's MIT-licensed reader ([bsb_read.c](https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/blob/master/frmts/bsb/bsb_read.c), [bsbdataset.cpp](https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/blob/master/frmts/bsb/bsbdataset.cpp)):

- A text header of keyword lines (`VER/`, `BSB/` or `NOS/`, `KNP/` with `PR=` projection and `GD=` datum, `RGB/` palette entries, `REF/n,pixel,line,lat,long` reference points, `PLY/n,lat,long` border polygon, optional `WPX/PWX/WPY/PWY` polynomials, `DTM/` datum shift), ended by `0x1A 0x00`.
- One byte of colour depth, then run-length rows: each row starts with a 7-bit varint row number; each run byte carries the colour index in its top `nColorSize` bits and the count in the rest, continued by bytes with the high bit set. A trailing index table of row offsets gives random access by row.
- GDAL handles Mercator, Transverse Mercator, UTM, Polyconic and Lambert Conformal Conic and derives a geotransform from the `REF` points; it does not apply the `WPX/PWX` polynomials. OpenCPN's GPL-2.0 `chartimg.cpp` parses `WPX`/`PWX` and uses them when present ([chartimg.cpp](https://github.com/OpenCPN/OpenCPN/blob/master/gui/src/chartimg.cpp)).
- GDAL's header says the format "is subject to US patent 5,727,090; however, that patent apparently only covers *writing*". A patent granted in 1998 has expired **(estimate from the 20-year term; grant date not verified)**.
- GDAL also offers alternative palettes (`DAY`, `DSK`, `NGT`, `NGR`, `GRY`) via `BSB_PALETTE` ([GDAL BSB driver](https://gdal.org/en/stable/drivers/raster/bsb.html)), which maps directly onto the app's night mode.
- Cost for this app: a header parser, an RLE decoder, `REF` to Mercator affine fit, and resampling into 256 px Web Mercator tiles at import. A few hundred lines plus tests **(estimate)**. Polyconic and LCC charts need inverse projections, which `proj4rs` provides.

**Image codecs.** `image` 0.24.9 (2024-02-23) decodes WebP in pure Rust but needs libwebp (C) to encode, and decodes JPEG with `jpeg-decoder` ([image 0.24.9 README](https://github.com/image-rs/image/blob/v0.24.9/README.md)). `image` 0.25.0 "switched to image-webp for WebP encoding and to zune-jpeg for JPEG decoding" and 0.25.4 brought "much faster decoding of lossless WebP" ([image CHANGES.md](https://github.com/image-rs/image/blob/v0.25.10/CHANGES.md)). In 0.25.10 the default features include `jpeg` (zune-jpeg), `png` and `webp` (image-webp); AVIF *encoding* is pure Rust (`ravif`), but AVIF *decoding* needs `avif-native`, which is the C library dav1d (crates.io feature list). `image-webp` decodes lossy and lossless at "70-100% of the speed of libwebp" and contains no unsafe code; its encoder is lossless only ([image-webp](https://github.com/image-rs/image-webp)). `jpeg-decoder` is in maintenance mode ([jpeg-decoder](https://github.com/image-rs/jpeg-decoder)).

Notes on the rows:

- **MBTiles 1.3** has a `tiles` table or view keyed by `zoom_level, tile_column, tile_row` in TMS order, plus `metadata`. `format` must be one of `pbf`, `jpg`, `png`, `webp` or an IETF media type; `bounds`, `center`, `minzoom`, `maxzoom` are SHOULD; vector tilesets MUST carry a `json` row with `vector_layers` ([MBTiles 1.3 spec](https://github.com/mapbox/mbtiles-spec/blob/master/1.3/spec.md)). SQLite gives many concurrent readers and one writer. Size overhead over raw tiles is small (page slack and the index) **(estimate; no published figure found)**. Deduplication of identical tiles (open sea) needs the optional `map`/`images` split, which the spec allows because `tiles` may be a view.
- **OpenCPN.** The wiki says OpenCPN "supports the use of MBTiles (v5.0.0)" ([OpenCPN MBTiles wiki](https://opencpn.org/wiki/dokuwiki/doku.php?id=opencpn%3Amanual_advanced%3Acharts%3Ambtiles)). Its source sets `m_ChartFamily = CHART_FAMILY_RASTER`, reads `format` and the `scheme` (`XYZ` or default TMS) from metadata, and decodes tiles with `wxBITMAP_TYPE_ANY` ([mbtiles.cpp](https://github.com/OpenCPN/OpenCPN/blob/master/gui/src/mbtiles/mbtiles.cpp)), so it is raster only. WebP tiles depend on wxWidgets' image handlers **(unverified)**. The wiki notes MBTiles "only show at certain scales" and are not quilted like charts.
- **Signal K.** `@signalk/charts-plugin` 3.8.2 serves "MBTiles files", TMS directories, XYZ directories with `metadata.json` and online TMS, and accepts manual entries for WMS, WMTS, S-57 tiles and TileJSON ([charts-plugin README](https://github.com/SignalK/charts-plugin), [package.json](https://github.com/SignalK/charts-plugin/blob/master/package.json)). PMTiles needs `signalk-pmtiles-plugin` (Apache-2.0, 1.2.2) ([panaaj/signalk-pmtiles-plugin](https://github.com/panaaj/signalk-pmtiles-plugin)) or `signalk-charts-provider-simple`, which handles "MBTiles and PMTiles (raster and vector)" ([KEGustafsson/signalk-charts-provider-simple](https://github.com/KEGustafsson/signalk-charts-provider-simple)) **(secondary)**.
- **PMTiles v3**: a 127-byte header, root directory "MUST be contained in the first 16,384 bytes", JSON metadata, leaf directories, tile data; tile IDs follow a Hilbert curve and a "clustered" archive stores tile data in tile-ID order ([PMTiles v3 spec](https://github.com/protomaps/PMTiles/blob/main/spec/v3/spec.md)). Adding one tile means rewriting directories, so updates are a rebuild. That suits an import step that writes once, and is awkward for a chart that is patched in place. Tippecanoe notes `--allow-existing` does not work with PMTiles output ([Protomaps: creating PMTiles](https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/create)) **(secondary)**.
- **GeoPackage** is the right shape for a whole-chart vector file (features plus an R-tree), but nothing in the boat ecosystem reads it, and on device it still has to be rasterised per frame.
- **Directory of files** on an SD card: millions of small files are slow to copy and waste space at the 4 KiB cluster size **(estimate)**; SAS.Planet itself moved to an SQLite cache to avoid "millions of individual image files" **(secondary, same help source)**.
- **MOBAC** 2.4.0 (2026-10-19) reads SQLite atlases including MBTiles ([MOBAC](https://mobac.sourceforge.io/), [README](https://mobac.sourceforge.io/MOBAC/README.html)) and writes MBTiles among its atlas formats ([MOBAC atlas formats](https://mobac.sourceforge.io/wiki/index.php/Atlas_formats)) **(page not fetched)**.

## 4. Raster tile encoding on a Pi

- **No published benchmark of 256 px tile decoding on a Raspberry Pi was found.** The numbers below are from desktop or mobile and only rank the codecs.
- libwebp maintainers' rule of thumb: lossy WebP "1.5 slower to decode" than JPEG, varying with image size ([webp-discuss](https://groups.google.com/a/webmproject.org/g/webp-discuss/c/hn3LwO6_bS0)) **(secondary)**.
- A Rust project measured pure-Rust decoders at 640x360: JPEG 0.79 ms, PNG 1.61 ms, WebP 3.45 ms; at 1280x720 the Rust decoders took 1.78 ms for JPEG and 5.66 ms for WebP against 0.72 ms and 1.95 ms for the C libraries ([vaam-apps/image-resizer #166](https://github.com/vaam-apps/image-resizer/issues/166)) **(secondary)**.
- zune-jpeg aims to be "as fast as libjpeg-turbo", with intrinsics for "almost" matching it ([zune-jpeg README](https://github.com/etemesi254/zune-image/tree/dev/crates/zune-jpeg)). `zune-png` 0.5.2 is a separate fast PNG decoder, not the one `image` uses (`image` uses `png` 0.18.1).
- Scaling those figures by pixel count, a 256 px tile is about 0.28 of a 640x360 image, so roughly 0.2 ms JPEG, 0.45 ms PNG and 1 ms lossy WebP on a desktop core, and perhaps two to four times that on a Pi 4 or 5 core **(estimate)**. All three fit on the tile-loader thread; WebP costs the most CPU per tile.
- **Scanned charts** (BSB) have at most 127 palette colours by construction (7-bit indices in the RLE), so paletted PNG with no dithering is lossless and small; JPEG smears thin linework and soundings. For harvested imagery, JPEG or lossy WebP is the right choice **(estimate based on content type)**.
- **256 vs 512 px**: 512 px tiles quarter the tile count and SQLite lookups per screen and compress slightly better, but every tile decoded costs four times as much, which matters on a cold cache **(estimate)**. 256 px is what NOAA NCDS, SAS.Planet and QGIS's XYZ tool produce by default **(unverified for NCDS)**; keeping 256 px avoids resampling users' existing files.
- **Hi-DPI**: a 512 px tile can be drawn as a 256 px tile at 2x for a dense screen; for scanned charts this only helps if the scan is finer than the zoom level's resolution **(estimate)**.

## 5. Vector charts on the device

- **Tiled MVT in MBTiles or PMTiles** keeps the existing tile pipeline (fetch bytes by z/x/y on the loader thread), bounds the work per tile, and is what Freeboard-SK uses for S-57 ("S57 ENC's (converted to vector tiles)") ([Freeboard-SK wiki](https://github.com/SignalK/freeboard-sk/wiki)) **(secondary)**. Cost: rasterising each tile (sibling study estimates 20 to 40 ms per full redraw with `tiny-skia` on an A76), and S-52-style styling written against tile attributes. MVT tiles quantise to a 4,096-unit extent by default ([GDAL PMTiles driver](https://gdal.org/en/stable/drivers/vector/pmtiles.html)).
- **Single whole-chart vector file** (FlatGeobuf or GeoPackage with a spatial index): exact geometry, no generalisation, simple to write from Rust (`flatgeobuf`, `geopackage`). The renderer must then query by bounding box and simplify per zoom itself on every frame or cache, which is the work tippecanoe does once at build time. No boat software consumes it, so no interoperability.
- **Pre-rasterised** (vector rendered to raster MBTiles at import): zero runtime cost and readable by OpenCPN and Signal K as they are; NOAA NCDS is exactly this. Freezes styling, safety contour and language at import (sibling study, Route C).
- **Tippecanoe** generalises by dropping or coalescing features to keep tiles under 500 KB (`--drop-densest-as-needed`, `--coalesce-smallest-as-needed` and others) and simplifies lines and polygons ([tippecanoe README](https://github.com/felt/tippecanoe)); Felt maintains it, it reads GeoJSON, FlatGeobuf and CSV and writes MBTiles or PMTiles. It is C++, so it is an external tool.
- **GDAL 3.8+** writes MVT to PMTiles directly with a `SIMPLIFICATION` option and multi-threaded conversion ([GDAL PMTiles](https://gdal.org/en/stable/drivers/vector/pmtiles.html)); also external.
- **A pure-Rust tiler.** No crate found that does what tippecanoe does end to end **(unverified beyond crates.io search of the names listed)**. The parts exist: clip with `i_overlay` or `clipper2`, simplify and project with `geo`, encode with `mvt` or `geozero`, write with `pmtiles` or SQLite. For chart-sized inputs (one S-57 cell, a regional seamark extract) a simple per-tile clip plus Douglas-Peucker is practical; planet-scale feature dropping is not needed **(estimate)**. `versatiles` (pure Rust, 5.13.0) converts and repairs existing tile containers but does not tile raw vectors from scratch **(unverified)**.
- **MapLibre Tile (MLT)** was announced on 2026-01-23 as a column-oriented successor with "feature parity with MVT" and "compression up to 6x on large tiles"; GL JS reads it from 5.12.0 ([MapLibre MLT announcement](https://maplibre.org/news/2026-01-23-mlt-release/), [implementation status](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-tile-spec/implementation-status/)) **(secondary)**. The Rust `mlt` crate is 0.7.0 and two days old at the time of writing; Freeboard-SK, the Signal K plugins and OpenCPN have no MLT support found. MVT is the safe choice for now.

## 6. What Signal K, Freeboard-SK and OpenCPN do for free worldwide charts

- **Freeboard-SK** has "Built in support (no plugin required) for OpenStreetMap and OpenSeaMap" and gets every other chart from the server's `/resources/charts` API ([Freeboard-SK README](https://github.com/SignalK/freeboard-sk)). Supported chart types: XYZ image tiles, MVT/PBF, S-57 converted to vector tiles, WMS, WMTS and PMTiles ([Freeboard-SK wiki](https://github.com/SignalK/freeboard-sk/wiki)) **(secondary)**. Only unencrypted ENCs; no S-63 **(secondary)**.
- **`@signalk/charts-plugin`** lists as free MBTiles sources: NOAA nautical charts (NCDS), Finnish Transport Agency charts ([vokkim/rannikkokartat-mbtiles](https://github.com/vokkim/rannikkokartat-mbtiles)) and the Signal K World Coastline Map ([charts-plugin README](https://github.com/SignalK/charts-plugin)).
- **Other Signal K plugins**: `signalk-charts-provider-simple` converts uploaded S-57 `.000` files to vector tiles in a Docker or Podman container; `signalk-seamap-plugin` serves sector-based seamap PMTiles; `signalk-chart-locker` runs a Rust container for a boat-wide tile cache and PMTiles ([search summary](https://github.com/NearlCrews/signalk-chart-locker), [prozessor13/signalk-seamap-plugin](https://github.com/prozessor13/signalk-seamap-plugin)) **(secondary)**.
- **OpenCPN chart downloader** catalogues ([chartcatalogs/catalogs](https://github.com/chartcatalogs/catalogs), last commit 2026-11-24 "Automatic catalog update"). The 27 catalogues: Argentina RNC (archived), Brazil RNC and IENC, Peru RNC, New Zealand RNC (archive), NOAA NCDS MBTiles (46 files), GEBCO shaded-relief MBTiles, GSHHG and OSM world basemaps, US pilot charts, South China Sea ENC, US Army Corps buoy cells, and inland IENC for Austria, Bulgaria, Switzerland, Czechia, Germany, EURIS, France (two), Croatia, Hungary, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Serbia and Slovakia. The free *sea* charts outside the Americas and NZ in that list amount to 58 small-scale South China Sea cells.
- **Pattern across the three**: free charts are moved around as MBTiles (raster) for display, and as S-57 converted to MVT where vector is wanted. PMTiles is gaining ground in Signal K plugins but is not yet in the core charts plugin or OpenCPN.

## Recommendation

Written against this codebase at `d475fe0` and the sibling S-57 document. The product assumption is the one given: an amateur, worldwide leisure sailor, a Raspberry Pi at the chart table, charts that are free or cheap, pre-processing on import accepted, no external dependencies at runtime or import, and a loop that stays responsive.

### 1. Keep MBTiles as the one on-device format, for raster and for vector

The doubt about MBTiles is reasonable, but the evidence points the other way for this product.

- **It is already the fast path.** `TileStore` fetches a tile blob from SQLite in microseconds (`bench_` figures in the repo), the LRU and the loader thread are built around `(z, x, y)` blobs, and the warm frame is 3 ms. There is no performance to win from another container; the cost is the PNG decode, not the fetch.
- **No new dependency.** `rusqlite` with the bundled SQLite is already compiled in. PMTiles would add a crate, GeoPackage would add schema handling for no gain, a bespoke format would add code and remove every tool.
- **Incremental writes make the import step simple.** An importer can `INSERT` tiles as it produces them, one transaction per batch, resume after a power cut, and append zoom levels later. PMTiles must be written in one sorted pass once every tile exists; that is why its producers are converters, not importers.
- **It is the lingua franca of the hobby.** SAS.Planet, MOBAC, QGIS, MapTiler, tippecanoe and GDAL write it; OpenCPN and the Signal K charts plugin read it. A sailor's existing collection (the satellite and Navionics-derived sets already in `map/`) works unchanged, and a chart made for this app can be copied to OpenCPN or Freeboard on the same boat. Interoperability is worth more to an amateur than any format elegance.
- **Vector tiles fit in the same file.** MBTiles 1.3 carries gzip-compressed Mapbox Vector Tiles with `format = pbf` and a `json` metadata key listing the layers. A vector chart is therefore "an MBTiles whose tiles need rasterising", which is exactly the adapter shape the S-57 document describes beneath `TileStore`. A whole-chart vector file (FlatGeobuf, GeoPackage features) would instead need generalisation at runtime on every zoom, which is the wrong place to do it on a Pi.

Two consequences to adopt now: treat the MBTiles **metadata table as part of the format** (`name`, `bounds`, `minzoom`, `maxzoom`, `format`, and for vector `json`), because chart selection by coverage (section 3 below) depends on it; and **read PMTiles when it costs one adapter** (pure-Rust `pmtiles` 0.28, which also writes), because Freeboard-side tools and new free sources are starting to ship it. Do not use it as the on-device store: it is written once and finalised, so an importer cannot add a zoom level or resume after a power cut, and OpenCPN and the core Signal K charts plugin do not read it.

### 2. Target compatibility, in tiers

**Tier 0, native: MBTiles.** Raster tiles PNG or JPEG (WebP once the `image` crate upgrade is taken), vector tiles MVT. The app reads this; everything else is imported into it.

**Tier 1, import (pure Rust, no runtime cost), in this order of value for a worldwide amateur:**

1. **MBTiles and PMTiles pass-through.** Validate, fix metadata, note TMS versus XYZ, copy into `map/`. Cheap, and it makes the catalogue (section 3) honest. This is also how the best free official raster arrives: NOAA cancelled its BSB charts in December 2024 but publishes 46 ENC-derived raster MBTiles through its Chart Display Service.
2. **BSB/KAP raster charts.** Still the format of LINZ, Brazil and archived Argentine rasters, and of twenty years of charts sailors already own; NOAA's RNC page is gone, so new BSB supply is shrinking. No Rust reader exists. The format is small (a text header, an RLE-encoded 8-bit palette image, calibration points and polynomials); a reader is a few hundred lines of Rust (estimate), with GDAL's MIT-licensed driver as the reference, and the import warps it into Web Mercator tiles with nearest or bilinear sampling and writes paletted PNG. Start with Mercator-projected charts, which are the vast majority, and refuse the others with a clear message until transverse Mercator and polyconic are added.
3. **GeoTIFF.** LINZ and other offices publish rasters this way, and anyone who georeferences a scanned chart in QGIS gets one. The `tiff` crate reads the pixels; the GeoKeys give the projection; the same warp-to-tiles code as BSB produces the output. Accept Web Mercator and geographic (EPSG:4326) first.
4. **A free world base chart built from open data.** OSM land polygons and coastline (shapefile, ODbL), OpenSeaMap seamarks (OSM PBF, ODbL), GEBCO_2026 (15 arc-second grid) or EMODnet (about 115 m, Europe) bathymetry turned into depth contours (GeoTIFF plus marching squares), tiled with the pure-Rust pieces the research found (`geo` simplification, `i_overlay` clipping, the `mvt` writer); no tippecanoe equivalent exists in Rust, so per-zoom generalisation is the part to write. This is the feature that makes "worldwide and free" true: every user gets coast, lights, buoys, harbours and depth context everywhere, legally (ODbL and GEBCO terms with attribution), with an on-screen caveat that it is not survey data. It is a laptop job measured in hours for the planet; the output is one MBTiles the project can publish.
5. **S-57 ENC.** For the free official cells (NOAA, LINZ, Brazil, Peru, Argentine archives, the 27 mostly inland sources in the OpenCPN downloader catalogue). Requires writing the S-57 reader; see the sibling document. It feeds the same style engine as item 4.

**Ruled out:** CM93 (proprietary, no legal source for a consumer), oeSENC, Navionics and S-63 (encrypted by design), and any built-in harvesting of satellite or Navionics web tiles: Google, Bing and Esri terms forbid offline caching. The app reads such files when a user supplies them, as it does today, but should not be the tool that makes them. For Portugal, Madeira and the Azores the cheap legal chart is o-charts at about 16 EUR, which only OpenCPN can open; the honest answer for those waters is the open-data base chart plus OpenCPN alongside. MapLibre Tiles and other new encodings until a pure-Rust reader is mature.

### 3. Three runtime changes that matter more than any format

- **A chart catalogue instead of a file list.** Read each MBTiles' metadata at startup into `{name, bounds, zoom range, kind}`; show names, not file stems; and offer "best chart here" (smallest-scale chart whose bounds contain the view, at a zoom it has), which is what a sailor with thirty files actually wants. Raster quilting across files, OpenCPN style, is a later step; selection by coverage is the first and needs no new format, only metadata discipline in the importer.
- **One style engine, used offline first.** Write the feature-to-drawing rules (a reduced S-52 for ENC, a simple style for OSM) as a pure module that produces draw commands, and rasterise with `tiny-skia` **in the importer**, producing raster MBTiles. The Pi's runtime then changes not at all (route C of the S-57 document). Moving that same engine on-device behind a `TileSource` adapter later gives true overzoom, the S-52 night palette and a feature pick (route B), as an optimisation rather than a prerequisite. Two consumers of one module is the deletion-test pass.
- **The importer lives in the same crate.** A second binary target or a `plotter import <file>` subcommand sharing the tile, projection and style modules, so there is still one thing to build and one set of tests. It runs on the laptop for big jobs and on the Pi for a single chart.

### 4. Performance implications on the Pi

| Step | Where | Cost (estimate unless stated) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading any raster MBTiles | runtime | unchanged: 3.1 ms warm frame (measured) |
| Reading a PMTiles | runtime | same order; one more adapter, no SQLite |
| BSB/GeoTIFF import of one chart (10k x 8k px, z8-z15) | importer | tens of seconds to a few minutes on a Pi 4, dominated by PNG encoding; seconds on a laptop |
| World base chart build (planet) | laptop only | hours; ship the result |
| S-57 to raster tiles for a region | laptop, later Pi | minutes per cell set |
| On-device vector rasterisation (if ever) | runtime loader thread | 3-20 ms per tile on a Pi 4, see the sibling document |

Nothing in tiers 0-1 touches the 16 ms loop. The only runtime work is the catalogue read at startup (one SQLite query per file, milliseconds) and the optional PMTiles adapter.

### 5. Suggested order

1. Catalogue from metadata, names in the menu, best-chart-here. Small, immediate, and it fixes the current clipped-filename menus.
2. `plotter import` with pass-through and BSB/KAP, then GeoTIFF. The worldwide free rasters become usable.
3. Style engine plus the open-data world base chart, rasterised offline. Worldwide free coverage.
4. S-57 reader feeding the same engine, offline. Free ENCs where they exist.
5. On-device vector tiles, only if overzoom, night palette or feature pick prove worth the loader-thread cost measured on the real Pi.
